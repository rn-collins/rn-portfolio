SCENE 1
BUILD 053. ONE DIFFERENT DAY IS NOT AN EXPERIMENT. A change in light, sound, layout, nature, timing, task, or expectation can move with the outcome and make a simple before-and-after story misleading.

SCENE 2
DECLARE THE PROTOCOL. CONDITION · SEQUENCE · MEASURE · STOP. Use an exact-bound synthetic protocol. Name the comparison, repeated observations, timing, confounders, missing data, human control, and conditions that end the test.

SCENE 3
REPAIR THE DESIGN. ALTERNATE · REPEAT · UNBLOCK. Replace a blocked, confounded sequence with a longer alternating one. Recompute only descriptive condition means, their difference, and declared design features.

SCENE 4
READ STRENGTH CAREFULLY. WEAK · LIMITED · PATTERN TO RETEST. Strength reflects sequence, repetition, and confounding—not an effect, causal conclusion, health measure, universal response, or proof the environment produced change.

SCENE 5
BOUND THE RESULT. DESCRIPTION IS NOT PRESCRIPTION. The demo cannot conduct research, diagnose, treat, measure cognition or exposure, determine safety or accessibility, infer a population effect, or recommend a real environmental change.

SCENE 6
SAVE THIS. MAKE THE PROTOCOL VISIBLE. A TINY EXPERIMENT BECOMES USEFUL WHEN THE PROTOCOL MAKES ALTERNATIVES, REPETITION, CONFOUNDING, AND UNCERTAINTY VISIBLE.
