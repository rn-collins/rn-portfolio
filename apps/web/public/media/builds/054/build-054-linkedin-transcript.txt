SCENE 1
BUILD 054. A ROOM CAN ADAPT WITHOUT DECIDING FOR A PERSON. Light, quiet zones, and seating can be proposed from a synthetic task and context—but a configuration is not permission to change a real space.

SCENE 2
DECLARE THE SCENARIO. PERSON · TASK · CONTEXT · CONSTRAINT. Use only an exact-bound synthetic scenario. Keep occupants, task, preferences, conflicts, unknowns, authority, and available controls visible.

SCENE 3
RECOMPUTE THE PROPOSAL. SETTING · RATIONALE · TRADEOFF. When the scenario changes, recompute proposed light, quiet-zone, and seating settings. Preserve unresolved conditions and explicit non-actions.

SCENE 4
MAKE CONFLICT REVIEWABLE. ONE PERSON · MANY PEOPLE · HUMAN REVIEW. A setting that supports one task may complicate another. Do not collapse competing needs into a score, prediction, inferred trait, or automatic accommodation.

SCENE 5
BOUND THE SYSTEM. PROPOSE—DO NOT ACTUATE. The demo reads no sensors, controls no equipment, measures no effect, infers no protected trait, and cannot determine safety, accessibility, compliance, or personal need.

SCENE 6
SAVE THIS. KEEP THE PERSON IN CONTROL. AN ADAPTIVE SPACE IS ACCOUNTABLE ONLY WHEN PROPOSALS, TRADEOFFS, AUTHORITY, CONSENT, OVERRIDE, AND FALLBACK REMAIN VISIBLE.
