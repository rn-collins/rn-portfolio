SCENE 1
BUILD 050. A ROOM IS NOT A NEUTRAL CONTAINER. Light, sound, layout, temperature, nature, crowding, timing, task, and control may shape experience—but the same condition does not affect every person or goal the same way.

SCENE 2
READ THE CONDITIONS. LIGHT · SOUND · LAYOUT · NATURE. Use exact-bound synthetic rooms. Keep each feature visible with setting, goal, duration, available control, uncertainty, and what has not actually been measured.

SCENE 3
CHANGE ROOM OR GOAL. THE INTERPRETATION MUST MOVE. When a room or goal changes, recompute only the declared model. Preserve competing needs, interactions, unknowns, and the possibility that one feature helps one goal while complicating another.

SCENE 4
SHOW EVIDENCE STRENGTH. MODERATE · LIMITED · UNKNOWN. An evidence label must expose scope and uncertainty. It cannot turn an illustrative model, association, preference, or single experience into a causal or universal conclusion.

SCENE 5
BOUND THE RESULT. MODEL ≠ MEASUREMENT ≠ PRESCRIPTION. A synthetic load cannot diagnose harm, measure exposure, prove causation, determine accessibility or compliance, represent every person, or prescribe a real design.

SCENE 6
SAVE THIS. READ THE ROOM WITH THE PERSON. A SPACE BECOMES LEGIBLE WHEN CONDITIONS, EXPERIENCE, EVIDENCE STRENGTH, AND TRADEOFFS CAN BE READ TOGETHER.
