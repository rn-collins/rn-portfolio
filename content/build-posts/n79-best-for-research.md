## LINKEDIN TEXT POST

The AI research tool that sounds the most confident is often the one most likely to make something up.

I built a free guide that tells you which one to actually use — and for what.

The problem:
You need to research something. You open ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, whatever. You get an answer fast. It sounds authoritative. You use it. Then you follow a citation and it says something completely different, or doesn't exist at all. For quick trivia this is fine. For client work, a thesis, or a published piece — it's a real problem.

What I built:
**Best AI Tools for Research** — a free decision guide inside Aloha AI University.

What it covers:
→ Perplexity vs. Claude vs. ChatGPT with browsing vs. Consensus vs. Elicit — when each one is right
→ Hallucination risk: which tools have which failure modes (and how to catch them)
→ Use-case fit matrix: competitive research, academic lit, deep dives, client briefings
→ When to chain tools — and what that workflow actually looks like
