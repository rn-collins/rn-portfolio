# 100 Builds Replacement Tournament — Round 2

## Purpose
Round 2 converts the strategic audit from generic scorekeeping into a public-canon selection mechanism. A numbered slot must earn public attention; necessary engineering infrastructure does not automatically deserve a public build number.

## Tournament rule
A challenger replaces an incumbent when it is materially stronger across the combined objective: RN fit × audience demand × saveability × visual potential × utility/adoption × technical progression × distinctiveness × click potential × commercial gravity, while preserving or improving the engineering capability sequence.

## Confirmed demotions to RN Lab infrastructure
These remain mandatory implementation work but no longer deserve public slots in generic form:
- Identity & Authentication Layer
- Data Ingestion & Connector Framework
- API Gateway & Integration Layer
- Deployment, Reliability & Observability System

## Round 2 challengers

### AI Implementation Readiness X-Ray
**Problem:** organizations buy AI before the workflow, evidence, permissions, review, integration, adoption, and measurement conditions exist.
**A:** structured diagnostic producing readiness map, blockers, sequencing recommendations, and implementation preconditions.
**B:** a shiny AI layer attempts to land on an organization and falls through missing structural layers.
**Why RN:** bridges AI implementation, governance, workflow design, legal/regulatory constraints, human factors, and organizational systems.
**Technical capability:** scoring engine, conditional recommendations, dependency graph, report generation.
**Commercial path:** executive/innovation/legal/operations teams can recognize a paid readiness/scoping engagement.

### Policy-to-Workflow Translator
**Problem:** enacted policy and written rules do not explain what operationally changes Monday morning.
**A:** convert policy text into actors, triggers, obligations, deadlines, evidence, approvals, exceptions, handoffs, and unresolved implementation questions.
**B:** a block of policy text physically unfolds into a live operational system.
**Why RN:** law + implementation + systems architecture.
**Technical capability:** structured extraction, workflow schema, state model, evidence mapping.
**Commercial path:** legal ops, government, regulated companies, implementation consulting.

### Creator Ownership Stack Mapper
**Problem:** independent operators often cannot see which parts of their economic infrastructure they own versus rent.
**A:** map audience, IP, data, distribution, relationships, archives, workflows, revenue, platforms, and dependencies into owned / portable / rented / exposed layers.
**B:** a creator standing alone gradually reveals the institutional stack surrounding them; rented layers flicker or disappear.
**Why RN:** Institutions of One + IP + creator systems + entrepreneurship.
**Technical capability:** dependency graph, ownership classification, risk scoring, recommendation engine.
**Commercial path:** founders, creators, portfolio professionals, platforms and sponsors.

### AI Vendor Claim / Procurement X-Ray
**Problem:** procurement decisions rely on claims whose evidence, data flows, human controls, dependencies and implementation assumptions are difficult to compare.
**A:** evaluate vendor claims against evidence, data provenance, controls, lock-in, integration, legal exposure, security and unanswered questions.
**B:** a glossy product claim is scanned layer-by-layer until the hidden dependencies appear.
**Why RN:** AI implementation + law + evidence + governance + procurement reasoning.
**Technical capability:** claim-evidence schema, vendor comparison, risk flags, evidence completeness.
**Commercial path:** legal, procurement, CIO/CTO, AI governance and advisory work.

### Evidence Strength & Claim Defensibility Mapper
**Problem:** people regularly say more than their evidence supports.
**A:** map a proposed claim to source type, quality, directness, uncertainty, applicability, contradictions and permissible strength of language.
**B:** one confident sentence is progressively weakened or strengthened as its evidence architecture appears.
**Why RN:** scientific training + legal reasoning + research/evidence infrastructure + journalism.
**Technical capability:** evidence ontology, claim scoring, provenance, recommendation rules.
**Commercial path:** research, health, legal, media, regulated products, AI evaluation.

### Institutional Friction Detector
**Problem:** recurring failure is often attributed to individuals when the institution has designed friction into the process.
**A:** diagnose handoffs, ambiguity, delays, duplicative work, cognitive load, authority gaps, inaccessible interfaces, conflicting incentives and escalation failures.
**B:** a person repeatedly collides with an invisible system until the institutional friction itself becomes visible.
**Why RN:** nervous-system-aware governance + law + human factors + systems analysis.
**Technical capability:** journey mapping, friction taxonomy, scoring, intervention prioritization.
**Commercial path:** public institutions, employers, education, legal operations, health systems.

### Neurotechnology Claim Audit
**Problem:** brain/neuroscience language can dramatically outrun mechanism and evidence.
**A:** inspect product/media claims against mechanism, measurement, population, effect size, uncertainty, replication, inference distance and practical significance.
**B:** a brain-shaped marketing claim is dissected into what was measured versus what was implied.
**Why RN:** unusually high RN-specificity through anatomy/neurobiology + research + law/regulation + technology.
**Technical capability:** claim-evidence graph, scientific inference-distance score, source provenance.
**Commercial path:** health tech, neurotech, wellness, media, regulatory/scientific diligence.

### Place-Sensitive Technology Readiness Tool
**Problem:** "works elsewhere" is routinely mistaken for implementation readiness.
**A:** test a technology against geography, infrastructure, workforce, legal regime, language, culture, accessibility, logistics, climate, public trust and local institutions.
**B:** a portable technology enters a map and changes shape as place-specific constraints appear.
**Why RN:** Hawaiʻi/place systems + technology implementation + governance.
**Technical capability:** localization matrix, constraint graph, readiness scoring.
**Commercial path:** Hawaiʻi/APAC/public-sector implementation, vendors entering new markets.

### Human Consequence Map for AI Workflows
**Problem:** technical architecture diagrams hide who experiences the consequences.
**A:** connect models, inputs, outputs and system actions to people, rights, decisions, downstream harms, recourse and review points.
**B:** a clean AI workflow flips over to reveal human consequence lines underneath it.
**Why RN:** AI + law + governance + neuroscience/human experience.
**Technical capability:** graph model, consequence taxonomy, review-gate mapping.
**Commercial path:** AI governance, legal, product, risk and policy teams.

### Third-Space / Future-of-Place Decision Lab
**Problem:** place concepts are often judged aesthetically or financially without integrating nervous-system effects, access, social connection, technology, governance and local context.
**A:** compare proposed spaces across social, sensory, accessibility, operational, economic, environmental, cultural and governance dimensions.
**B:** one empty room becomes multiple possible futures depending on whose nervous system, needs and incentives are centered.
**Why RN:** neuroaesthetics + place + governance + future-of-work + institutions.
**Technical capability:** multi-criteria decision model, scenario comparison, visual configuration.
**Commercial path:** real estate, hospitality, workplace, civic spaces, design and development.
