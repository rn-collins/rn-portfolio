# 100 Builds — Replacement Tournament, Round 1

Purpose: replace technically necessary but publicly weak slots with builds that still advance capability while creating stronger save/click/contact behavior and stronger RN fit.

## Locked rule
A public build must earn its numbered slot on nine dimensions: RN fit, audience demand, saveability, visual potential, utility/adoption, technical progression, distinctiveness, click potential, and commercial gravity. Necessary infrastructure that fails the public-value test moves to RN Lab infrastructure.

## First confirmed demotions to Lab infrastructure
- 021 Identity & Authentication Layer
- 025 Data Ingestion & Connector Framework
- 027 API Gateway & Integration Layer
- 034 Deployment, Reliability & Observability System

These capabilities remain mandatory in the engineering stack; they simply stop consuming public build slots.

## Round 1 challenger pool

### A. AI Vendor Claim / Procurement X-Ray
**Problem:** teams buy AI based on feature claims, security sheets, model cards, demos, and vague governance language that are difficult to compare.
**Functional build:** upload/paste vendor claims and evaluate evidence, data handling, model dependence, human controls, deployment constraints, lock-in, and unanswered questions.
**Visual build:** a glossy AI vendor promise is peeled apart into the evidence actually supporting it.
**Why RN:** AI implementation + governance + legal/regulatory thinking + procurement consequences.
**Capability advanced:** structured extraction, evidence scoring, comparison, claim provenance.
**Commercial gravity:** high; useful to firms, institutions, regulated teams, and buyers.

### B. Policy-to-Workflow Translator
**Problem:** policy, statute, guidance, or internal rules often stop at prose; teams still need to know who does what, when, with what evidence.
**Functional build:** turn a rule into actors, triggers, required actions, decisions, deadlines, evidence, approvals, and exceptions.
**Visual build:** a page of policy text physically unfolds into an operational workflow.
**Why RN:** law + systems architecture + AI implementation.
**Capability advanced:** structured parsing, workflow generation, state/handoff logic.
**Commercial gravity:** very high.

### C. Evidence Strength & Claim Defensibility Mapper
**Problem:** people routinely make stronger claims than their evidence supports.
**Functional build:** connect a proposed claim to evidence type, authority, quality, applicability, uncertainty, and defensible wording.
**Visual build:** one confident sentence is progressively weakened/qualified until it matches the evidence beneath it.
**Why RN:** researcher + neuroscientist + legal reasoning + evidence-heavy public work.
**Capability advanced:** provenance, scoring, claim transformation, source graph.
**Commercial gravity:** high for research, legal, policy, health, journalism, and AI teams.

### D. Institutional Friction Detector
**Problem:** organizations diagnose individual performance when the real failure is handoffs, incentives, process ambiguity, cognitive load, or contradictory rules.
**Functional build:** map a recurring breakdown and identify whether the friction is role, information, authority, timing, incentive, interface, or policy based.
**Visual build:** a person struggling inside a clean-looking process; the process becomes visible as the source of friction.
**Why RN:** psychology/neuroscience + governance + future of work + systems thinking.
**Capability advanced:** workflow analysis, causal tagging, recommendation logic.
**Commercial gravity:** high.

### E. Place-Sensitive Technology Readiness Tool
**Problem:** technologies are repeatedly treated as deployable everywhere despite infrastructure, geography, language, culture, law, labor, and access differences.
**Functional build:** evaluate a technology against place-specific implementation conditions.
**Visual build:** the same technology lands in three places and produces three different system diagrams.
**Why RN:** Hawaiʻi + Shanghai/international perspective + place-based technology + law/AI.
**Capability advanced:** localization schema, constraint modeling, scenario comparison.
**Commercial gravity:** high for public sector, infrastructure, health, education, and tech deployment.

### F. Neurotechnology Claim Audit
**Problem:** products use neuroscience language to imply mechanisms or outcomes beyond what evidence supports.
**Functional build:** inspect neuro/brain claims, claimed mechanism, evidence level, endpoint, population, and marketing leap.
**Visual build:** a brain-scan aesthetic dissolves into the actual evidence chain.
**Why RN:** neuroscience/anatomy + law/regulation + consumer technology.
**Capability advanced:** evidence extraction, claim verification, scientific/legal boundary logic.
**Commercial gravity:** high and distinctive.

### G. AI Workflow Human Consequence Map
**Problem:** teams diagram systems around software components rather than the humans affected when outputs move downstream.
**Functional build:** map model output → human decision → affected person/group → consequence → recourse.
**Visual build:** a technical architecture flips over to reveal the human consequence architecture underneath.
**Why RN:** AI + law + nervous-system-aware governance + human-centered systems.
**Capability advanced:** graph modeling, consequence propagation, recourse mapping.
**Commercial gravity:** high.

### H. Third-Space / Future-of-Place Decision Lab
**Problem:** decisions about third spaces, offices, campuses, retail, community places, and hybrid environments are made from utilization or aesthetics alone.
**Functional build:** compare a place concept across social connection, sensory load, accessibility, economics, safety, technology, governance, and local context.
**Visual build:** one floor plan changes as different human/system requirements are layered onto it.
**Why RN:** future of work + third spaces + neuroaesthetics + biophilic design + place-based tech.
**Capability advanced:** multi-criteria decision support, spatial data, scenario comparison.
**Commercial gravity:** high and aspirational.

### I. Creator Ownership Stack Mapper
**Problem:** independent creators increasingly operate like institutions but do not see the infrastructure they need to own: audience, IP, data, distribution, relationships, revenue, systems, archives.
**Functional build:** assess an independent operator's ownership stack and identify dependency risk.
**Visual build:** one creator gradually acquires the infrastructure of an institution.
**Why RN:** Institutions of One + creator economy + IP + future of work.
**Capability advanced:** dependency mapping, scoring, relationship graph, recommendation engine.
**Commercial gravity:** very high for Institutions of One.

### J. AI Implementation Readiness X-Ray
**Problem:** teams ask which AI tool to buy before determining whether the workflow, data, authority, review, security, and adoption conditions exist.
**Functional build:** diagnose implementation readiness around workflow clarity, data, human review, integration, permissions, risk, change management, and measurement.
**Visual build:** an AI button is pressed and the hidden prerequisites light up beneath it.
**Why RN:** directly positions AI implementation practice.
**Capability advanced:** readiness scoring, workflow/dependency modeling, prioritized remediation.
**Commercial gravity:** extremely high.

## Next round
Each challenger must be scored formally against the weakest surviving incumbent builds. Replacement occurs only when the challenger beats the incumbent on total public value while preserving or improving the capability progression. After replacement selection, the sequence is re-run from least to most technically complex; numbering is not preserved for sentimental reasons.
