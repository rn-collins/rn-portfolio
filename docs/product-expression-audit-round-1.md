# Product Expression Audit — Round 1

Purpose: begin the second-order audit. Strategic value answers whether a problem deserves a public slot. This audit asks whether the proposed A-side product is itself worthy of release under the rule: **maximum warranted technical sophistication behind the interface; minimum necessary cognitive burden in front of it.**

## 001 — Human Review Design Framework

**Audience-independent entry promise:** Tell us how human review works in one AI-assisted workflow. In about a minute, see whether the human can actually function as a safeguard and what to fix first.

**Primary novice path:** 60-second structured diagnostic; no login; no technical terms required; examples visible; optional free text only.

**Advanced path:** Design the Control exposes reviewer role, trigger, evidence, standard, authority, escalation, capacity, record and evaluation protocol.

**Technically ambitious version:** consequence-aware scoring; explainable dimension model; conditional recommendations; exportable protocol; scenario comparison; later provenance links to underlying governance sources; versioned methodology; optional team workflow mapping; no unnecessary backend for sensitive draft descriptions.

**Why software:** interactive diagnosis and consequence-aware prioritization materially outperform a static checklist.

**Current verdict:** APPROVE AS ANCHOR, still subject to usability/accessibility QA and evidence-traceability polish.

## AI Implementation Readiness X-Ray

**Plain-language promise:** Before you buy another AI tool, find out what has to be true for it to work in your organization.

**Novice path:** choose the workflow, people affected, data availability, review process, integrations and implementation constraints from guided choices; receive blockers and first three moves.

**Advanced path:** dependency graph, operating-model assumptions, role/authority matrix, risk tier, integration map, measurement plan and readiness evidence.

**Technically ambitious version:** dependency graph; conditional scoring; scenario simulator; workflow import; vendor/tool comparison; policy/evidence provenance; role graph; implementation sequence optimizer; exportable scoping brief.

**Do not require:** AI architecture vocabulary, API knowledge, model selection knowledge.

**Why software:** reveals hidden dependencies and computes sequencing across interacting readiness conditions.

**Verdict:** STRONG PUBLIC PRODUCT if the first-use path stays diagnostic rather than consultant-jargon heavy.

## Policy-to-Workflow Translator

**Plain-language promise:** Paste a rule. See what people actually have to do because of it.

**Novice path:** paste text or choose a sample; receive actors, actions, deadlines, approvals, evidence, exceptions and unanswered questions in plain language.

**Advanced path:** source citations, section-to-obligation provenance, workflow states, jurisdiction/effective-date metadata, change comparison and implementation checklist.

**Technically ambitious version:** structured legal/policy extraction; provenance-linked obligations; temporal/version logic; entity/actor resolution; workflow graph generation; human verification; diffing across amendments; export to task/workflow tools.

**Do not require:** legal training or workflow-modeling knowledge for the default path.

**Why software:** source-linked extraction and dynamic workflow generation make the rule operational rather than merely summarized.

**Verdict:** ANCHOR CANDIDATE.

## Creator Ownership Stack Mapper

**Plain-language promise:** Find out what parts of your independent business you actually own—and what could disappear if a platform changes the rules.

**Novice path:** guided inventory of audience, IP, data, distribution, relationships, archives, revenue and workflows; simple owned / portable / rented / exposed result.

**Advanced path:** dependency graph, concentration risk, portability plan, rights/document capture, replacement cost and migration roadmap.

**Technically ambitious version:** relationship/dependency graph; portability analysis; platform concentration scoring; IP/asset registry integration; scenario simulation for platform loss; prioritized independence roadmap.

**Do not require:** business-school, IP-law or technical vocabulary.

**Why software:** dependency and loss simulations transform a static checklist into an ownership-risk model.

**Verdict:** ANCHOR CANDIDATE, particularly strong Institutions-of-One fit.

## AI Vendor Claim / Procurement X-Ray

**Plain-language promise:** Paste what an AI vendor says its product can do. See what evidence and implementation questions are still missing before you rely on the claim.

**Novice path:** paste claim / upload public materials; receive claim, evidence found, missing proof, data questions, human-control questions, lock-in/integration questions and red flags.

**Advanced path:** vendor comparison, evidence provenance, contract-question generator, security/data-control matrix, implementation assumptions and unresolved risk register.

**Technically ambitious version:** document extraction; claim-evidence graph; source provenance; cross-document contradiction detection; vendor comparison model; evidence completeness scoring; retrieval across public/vendor materials; version tracking.

**Do not require:** procurement, security, ML or legal expertise to understand the primary output.

**Why software:** dynamic claim extraction and evidence linking are far more valuable than a procurement checklist.

**Verdict:** ANCHOR CANDIDATE.

## Evidence Strength & Claim Defensibility Mapper

**Plain-language promise:** Enter what you want to say. See how strongly the evidence actually lets you say it.

**Novice path:** claim + sources or sample evidence; receive supported / partly supported / inference / unsupported elements and safer wording.

**Advanced path:** source quality, directness, applicability, contradiction, freshness, inference distance, provenance and claim-version history.

**Technically ambitious version:** claim decomposition; evidence ontology; provenance graph; contradiction retrieval; calibrated language transformation; source/version tracking; domain-specific evaluation modules.

**Do not require:** research-methods or legal-evidence vocabulary.

**Why software:** claim decomposition and evidence-linked language calibration are interactive reasoning tasks.

**Verdict:** ANCHOR CANDIDATE and reusable infrastructure for many later builds.

## Institutional Friction Detector

**Plain-language promise:** Describe something that keeps going wrong at work. Find out whether the problem is really the person—or the system around them.

**Novice path:** guided incident/journey questions; output identifies likely friction types and first places to intervene.

**Advanced path:** handoff map, wait-state analysis, cognitive-load markers, authority gaps, conflicting incentives, policy/process mismatch and recurring-pattern comparison.

**Technically ambitious version:** process graph; friction taxonomy; event/journey modeling; recurrence clustering; intervention prioritization; team comparison; optional process-log import later.

**Do not require:** organizational-design terminology.

**Why software:** repeated-pattern and interaction analysis can expose systemic causes that a static worksheet cannot.

**Verdict:** STRONG, but must avoid pretending causal certainty from sparse self-report.

## Neurotechnology Claim Audit

**Plain-language promise:** Put a brain or cognition claim under a microscope. See what was actually measured and what the marketing language adds on top.

**Novice path:** paste claim + source/product page; receive measured outcome, population, mechanism claim, evidence level, inference leap and unanswered questions.

**Advanced path:** effect size, endpoint validity, replication, measurement limits, generalizability, uncertainty and regulatory/marketing flags where sourced.

**Technically ambitious version:** scientific claim extraction; study metadata retrieval; inference-distance model; provenance graph; evidence hierarchy; contradiction/replication search; claim-language comparison.

**Do not require:** neuroscience or statistics education to understand the primary result.

**Why software:** scientific evidence extraction and inference-distance analysis support a live claim audit.

**Verdict:** HIGHLY DISTINCTIVE ANCHOR CANDIDATE; accuracy standards must be unusually strict.

## Place-Sensitive Technology Readiness Tool

**Plain-language promise:** A technology can work somewhere and still fail here. Test what changes when place becomes part of the specification.

**Novice path:** choose technology + place; answer guided questions about connectivity, workforce, cost, infrastructure, language, law, climate, accessibility and trust; receive place-specific blockers and adaptations.

**Advanced path:** geospatial/contextual datasets, infrastructure dependencies, regulatory conditions, stakeholder map, localization requirements and scenario comparison across places.

**Technically ambitious version:** geospatial layers; place knowledge model; constraint graph; live public datasets where available; scenario engine; localization matrix; confidence/provenance for place assumptions.

**Do not require:** GIS, policy, infrastructure or engineering expertise.

**Why software:** place comparison, live data and constraint propagation create value unavailable in a static readiness checklist.

**Verdict:** SIGNATURE ANCHOR CANDIDATE.

## Human Consequence Map for AI Workflows

**Plain-language promise:** Draw the AI workflow. Then flip it over and see who experiences the consequences.

**Novice path:** guided steps through input → AI output → human action → affected person → consequence → recourse; visual map generated automatically.

**Advanced path:** rights/interests, severity, reversibility, review points, evidence, appeal/recourse, downstream propagation and stakeholder ownership.

**Technically ambitious version:** graph model; consequence propagation; scenario comparison; risk/control overlays; provenance; pathway simulation; exportable governance diagram.

**Do not require:** system architecture or risk-management vocabulary.

**Why software:** interactive graph construction and consequence propagation make hidden downstream effects visible.

**Verdict:** ANCHOR CANDIDATE.

---

## Third-Space / Future-of-Place Decision Lab

**Plain-language promise:** Show us a place you want to create or change. See how different design choices affect the people who will actually use it.

**Novice path:** choose place type and goals; structured questions about people, sensory conditions, access, social connection, safety, operations and local context; compare a few design scenarios.

**Advanced path:** evidence-linked environmental variables, N-of-1/user testing, spatial layers, accessibility, economics, technology, governance and scenario weighting.

**Technically ambitious version:** multi-criteria decision model; spatial configuration; evidence provenance; scenario simulator; preference weighting; later environmental sensor/N-of-1 integration; visualization of tradeoffs.

**Do not require:** architecture, neuroscience or planning education.

**Why software:** interactive scenario comparison and multi-criteria tradeoff visualization are central to the value.

**Verdict:** STRONG/ANCHOR CANDIDATE if methodology resists false precision.

# Round 1 conclusion

The build-level gate materially changes how the 100 should be specified. Public titles cannot merely name engineering capabilities. Each approved build now requires:
1. one-sentence plain-language promise;
2. default novice path;
3. optional advanced path;
4. most ambitious warranted technical architecture;
5. explicit 'do not require from user' constraints;
6. reason the artifact deserves to be software rather than static content;
7. accuracy/uncertainty guardrails;
8. A/B pair logic and conversion path.

Apply this structure to every surviving incumbent before the final canon is frozen.
