## Storyboard and on-screen transcript

The film is silent-first. All meaning is present as on-screen text; these six scenes run for three seconds each.

1. **ONE GENERIC CANNABIS LESSON IS NOT ENOUGH.** A question about basics, evidence, products, risk, or law begins in a different place—but personalization must not become medical or legal advice.
2. **QUESTION · EXPERIENCE · MISCONCEPTION.** Use an exact-bound synthetic learner. Keep jurisdiction and risk explicit, collect no real health profile, and preserve what the learner has not said.
3. **ORDER · DEPTH · CHECK · CORRECT.** Adapt only the curriculum sequence and knowledge checks. Show dated authoritative evidence, its limits, and the difference between association and causation.
4. **ESCALATE · CHECK CURRENT LAW.** Pregnancy, youth, poisoning, driving, interactions, severe symptoms, and legal questions route to current authoritative or professional help—not a personalized answer.
5. **EDUCATION IS NOT CLEARANCE.** The demo cannot diagnose, treat, recommend cannabis, choose a product or dose, predict impairment, establish safety, or state that a dated source answers every jurisdiction.
6. **TEACH THE PART THAT FITS.** THE RIGHT LESSON STARTS WITH THE QUESTION, PRESERVES UNCERTAINTY, AND STOPS WHERE EDUCATION BECOMES PERSONAL ADVICE.
