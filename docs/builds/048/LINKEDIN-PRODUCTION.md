## Storyboard and on-screen transcript

The film is silent-first. All meaning is present as on-screen text; these six scenes run for three seconds each.

1. **THE RULE AND THE JOURNEY ARE NOT THE SAME RECORD.** Formal language can describe eligibility, duties, steps, and remedies while hiding the time, cost, uncertainty, exposure, and effort required to navigate them.
2. **AUTHORITY · STEP · DECISION · RECOURSE.** Use a dated synthetic rule to map who acts, what is required, which evidence counts, where discretion enters, what notice is given, and how a decision can be challenged.
3. **TIME · COST · ACCESS · CONSEQUENCE.** Use synthetic experiences to preserve actual sequence, waiting, travel, language, disability, privacy, confusion, failed attempts, support, and unequal burden without claiming representativeness.
4. **PROPAGATE THE EFFECT.** When a step, document, deadline, channel, decision point, or recourse changes, recompute burden, access, failure points, notice, evidence, and downstream consequences.
5. **TEXT ≠ PRACTICE ≠ EXPERIENCE.** One rule, modeled path, or account cannot prove implementation, prevalence, fairness, causation, legal interpretation, remedy, or what every person experiences.
6. **READ THE RULE WITH THE JOURNEY.** A rule is not fully understood until its procedure, burden, experience, and recourse can be read together.

## Rights, privacy, security, and accessibility

Original deterministic typography and layout by Rayven-Nikkita Collins / RN Builds. No third-party footage, images, documents, music, voice, logos, or personal likenesses are used. All rules, procedures, decisions, people, accounts, journeys, burdens, and recourse paths described are synthetic. No legal, government, client, participant, health, identity, or case records are included. The film demonstrates a bounded comparison model and does not provide legal advice or establish current law, implementation, prevalence, fairness, causation, validity of an interpretation, availability of a remedy, or representative lived experience.

The silent-first composition does not require hearing. High-contrast on-screen text carries the complete narrative. The poster and ordered six-frame contact sheet provide static alternatives. The transcript above is the caption-equivalent record.
