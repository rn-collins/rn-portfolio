# Build 022 specification — Human Consequence Map for AI Workflows

## Canonical identity

- **022-A:** Human Consequence Map for AI Workflows
- **Canonical job:** Show who can be affected by an AI workflow and how consequences travel to them.
- **022-B Web:** Human impact nodes light up as workflow assumptions change.
- **Uses:** Builds 012, 013, and 017.
- **Creates:** cap:022.

These fields are recovered from the frozen repository canon. The public question, thesis, fixtures, formulas, thresholds, labels, and explanatory interface copy are derived implementation copy and are labeled accordingly.

## Derived public question and thesis

**Question:** Who experiences this workflow after its technical path appears to end?

**Thesis:** AI workflow consequences do not stop at a model output or institutional boundary. Direct subjects, households, workers, and communities need separate visibility, representation, notice, correction, and recourse.

## Functional behavior

022-A displays a synthetic AI-assisted benefits-triage workflow. A visitor can inspect four distinct affected groups, change whether each has meaningful notice, affected-person representation, and recourse, observe disclosed gaps, and export a portable JSON record locally. The engine concretely calls the Build 013 workflow scan (which inherits Build 012) and the Build 017 handoff assessment.

022-B is a separate interactive explanatory artifact. Changing one workflow assumption lights up additional human-impact nodes. Its sequence is illustrative, not a causal model.

## Evidence and limitations

NIST AI RMF 1.0 and the NIST AI RMF Playbook support mapping expected impacts to individuals, groups, communities, organizations, and society while involving relevant actors. The implementation-specific exposure signal is not a NIST score.

The map does not predict harm, establish causation, determine compliance, prove discrimination, decide eligibility, or measure lived experience. Direct research with affected people and competent domain review remain necessary.

## Integrity repair 022.2.0

A protection flag is not evidence. Every affected-person record now requires a unique identity, a recorded basis for the consequence path, and supporting evidence for notice, representation, and recourse before those protections can reduce the prioritization signal. Duplicate identities and unsupported flags remain explicit gaps. The synthetic workflow now includes the monitoring stage required by the inherited Build 013 scanner.
