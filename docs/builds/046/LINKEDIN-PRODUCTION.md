## Storyboard and on-screen transcript

The film is silent-first. All meaning is present as on-screen text; these six scenes run for three seconds each.

1. **ESSENTIAL SYSTEMS DO NOT END AT THE SHORE.** Food, water, energy, health, communications, transport, waste, finance, and public services depend on linked local capacity and external routes.
2. **SOURCE · ROUTE · SERVICE · PLACE.** Use synthetic infrastructure to connect each essential function to upstream supply, local assets, operators, alternatives, inventory, geography, and accountable owners.
3. **PORT · CABLE · ROUTE · SERVICE.** Remove a declared node or edge and trace immediate loss, delayed effects, cross-sector cascades, geographic reach, duration, uncertainty, and evidence limits.
4. **ACCESS · HEALTH · COST · MOBILITY.** Do not flatten island impact into one score. Preserve who is affected first, who has fewer substitutes, what burdens shift, and which communities define priority.
5. **FALL BACK · ALLOCATE · RESTORE · LEARN.** Compare bounded alternatives, prerequisites, tradeoffs, human authority, stop rules, restoration sequence, residual risk, and changes required before the next disruption.
6. **SEE THE SYSTEM BEFORE IT BREAKS.** Island resilience starts by making dependencies, cascades, unequal consequences, and recovery choices visible.

## Rights, privacy, security, and accessibility

Original deterministic typography and layout by Rayven-Nikkita Collins / RN Builds. No third-party footage, images, maps, music, voice, logos, or personal likenesses are used. The film demonstrates a bounded dependency model and does not establish real infrastructure configuration, vulnerability, capacity, readiness, priority, consequence, recovery time, public safety, or emergency guidance.
