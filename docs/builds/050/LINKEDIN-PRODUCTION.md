## Storyboard and on-screen transcript

The film is silent-first. All meaning is present as on-screen text; these six scenes run for three seconds each.

1. **A ROOM IS NOT A NEUTRAL CONTAINER.** Light, sound, layout, temperature, nature, crowding, timing, task, and control may shape experience—but the same condition does not affect every person or goal the same way.
2. **LIGHT · SOUND · LAYOUT · NATURE.** Use exact-bound synthetic rooms. Keep each feature visible with setting, goal, duration, available control, uncertainty, and what has not actually been measured.
3. **THE INTERPRETATION MUST MOVE.** When a room or goal changes, recompute only the declared model. Preserve competing needs, interactions, unknowns, and the possibility that one feature helps one goal while complicating another.
4. **MODERATE · LIMITED · UNKNOWN.** An evidence label must expose scope and uncertainty. It cannot turn an illustrative model, association, preference, or single experience into a causal or universal conclusion.
5. **MODEL ≠ MEASUREMENT ≠ PRESCRIPTION.** A synthetic load cannot diagnose harm, measure exposure, prove causation, determine accessibility or compliance, represent every person, or prescribe a real design.
6. **READ THE ROOM WITH THE PERSON.** A SPACE BECOMES LEGIBLE WHEN CONDITIONS, EXPERIENCE, EVIDENCE STRENGTH, AND TRADEOFFS CAN BE READ TOGETHER.
