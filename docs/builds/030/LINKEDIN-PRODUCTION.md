## On-screen transcript / captions

1. Passing a checklist is not an inclusive experience. A screen can satisfy isolated rules while real people still cannot perceive, understand, navigate, recover, or finish.
2. Test the whole journey: entry, orientation, action, feedback, error, recovery, completion, and the handoff beyond the interface.
3. Change the conditions: vision, hearing, motor, cognitive, speech, device, bandwidth, language, stress, interruption, and assistive technology.
4. Name where the task breaks: affected person, blocked action, evidence, consequence, workaround, owner, and severity—without impersonating lived experience.
5. Fix the system, not the person. Repair structure, language, control, feedback, timing, alternatives, and support; then retest.
6. Accessibility is not a view of the interface. It is whether people can complete the work.
