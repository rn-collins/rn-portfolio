# Homepage Comprehension Gate

Status: controlling public-surface QA

## Purpose
The Exhibition entrance is held to the same comprehension standard as every A-side build. The test is not whether a visitor is educated enough to understand the site; the test is whether the site unnecessarily requires specialized education, vocabulary, or domain knowledge.

## Controlling principle
Easy to enter. Deep enough to stay.

## Required first-screen comprehension
Within a few seconds, a first-time visitor should be able to answer:
1. Who is this? RN Collins.
2. What is here? 100 working tools/systems plus 100 visual explanations.
3. Why do these exist? RN notices missing systems/problems and builds responses to them.
4. What can I do next? Enter the 100, use a build, watch a visual build, or explore how the work connects.
5. Is this for me? No specialized background is required to enter or understand the basic proposition.

## Comprehension range used for testing, not segmentation
- early-secondary learner
- high-school learner
- general adult
- college-educated nonspecialist
- domain professional
- technical/research expert

There are not separate public versions for these groups. One interface must carry the same core meaning across the range. Depth is added through progressive disclosure.

## Language rules
- Explain before naming technical concepts.
- Prefer concrete actions over institutional abstractions.
- Do not lead with phrases such as interdisciplinary systems inquiry, infrastructure lineage, computational experimentation, accession architecture, or public laboratory unless plain meaning has already been established.
- Avoid insider language as navigation when ordinary language can do the same job.
- A technical term may appear later when it adds precision and is defined in context.

## Expert-depth rule
Plain language must not erase sophistication. Experts should be able to move deeper into methodology, research basis, architecture, evidence, limitations, technical implementation, and lineage.

## Failure conditions
The homepage fails if a visitor reasonably asks any of the following after the first screen:
- What does she actually build?
- Is this art, software, research, or consulting?
- What are A and B?
- What am I supposed to click?
- Do I need to understand technology or law to follow this?

## Acceptance test
A homepage may not be called public-ready until its copy and navigation pass the same comprehension-range review used for individual builds, including mobile and screen-reader order.
