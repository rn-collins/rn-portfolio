# Final Admission Records — Builds 041–050

Controlling standards: every public word must be comprehensible to a general visitor; expert terminology may appear secondarily; each A and B must reach its highest warranted technological ceiling.

## 041 — Who Owns the AI Work? — REFRAME Business AI Org Chart Generator
**Plain subtitle:** See who is responsible for each AI tool, decision, vendor, and workflow in an organization.
**Public question:** When AI is used at work, who actually owns what happens?
**Novice path:** Add teams, tools, vendors, and decisions. The system shows missing owners, unclear handoffs, and places where nobody can answer for the outcome.
**Expert depth:** decision rights, governance models, RACI/DACI-like structures, vendor accountability, escalation, policy ownership.
**A technological ceiling:** organization graph; workflow-to-owner mapping; vendor/tool registry; responsibility inference with human confirmation; conflict/orphan detection; policy and escalation overlays; graph queries; change history; simulation of role changes; explainable ownership gaps.
**B-Web ceiling:** interactive organizational topology where removing or reassigning one owner changes visible accountability paths in real time.
**B-LinkedIn:** an apparently organized company chart dissolves to reveal AI decisions nobody actually owns.

## 042 — Give the AI a Real Job — REFRAME AI Agent Job Design System
**Plain subtitle:** Define exactly what an AI agent may do, what it must not do, and when a person takes over.
**Public question:** If an AI agent were an employee, would anyone understand its job?
**Novice path:** Describe the job in ordinary language. Get a clear role, allowed tools, inputs, outputs, boundaries, review points, success measures, and stop conditions.
**Expert depth:** tool permissions, least privilege, authority scopes, evaluation criteria, prohibited actions, handoff contracts.
**A technological ceiling:** natural-language task decomposition; capability/tool matching; permission and policy graph; consequence-aware authority assignment; sandbox/tool simulations; job-spec validation; evaluation generation; conflict detection; versioning; human approval gates.
**B-Web ceiling:** drag responsibilities into/out of an AI agent's role and watch permissions, risks, human review, and evaluation requirements recompute.
**B-LinkedIn:** “AI assistant” expands into the dozens of unanswered questions hidden inside the job title.

## 043 — Design an AI Team — REFRAME Multi-Agent Organization Designer
**Plain subtitle:** Split a complicated job across specialized AI agents and people without losing control between them.
**Public question:** When one AI agent is doing too much, how should the work be divided?
**Novice path:** Describe a job. The system proposes understandable roles and shows who hands what to whom.
**Expert depth:** orchestration, shared memory, tool scopes, supervisor patterns, conflict resolution, termination conditions, agent communication protocols.
**A technological ceiling:** task graph decomposition; multi-agent topology optimizer; role specialization; shared-memory architecture; handoff contracts; conflict/deadlock detection; agent simulation; trace/evaluation integration; cost/latency modeling; human-control nodes; topology comparison.
**B-Web ceiling:** live multi-agent network where the visitor splits/merges roles and watches bottlenecks, failure paths, cost, and supervision change.
**B-LinkedIn:** one giant agent visibly overloads, fractures into specialized roles, then becomes governable.

## 044 — Reuse the Parts That Regulation Keeps Requiring — REFRAME Regulated Systems Library
**Plain subtitle:** Turn repeated requirements like approvals, consent, evidence, disclosures, and records into reusable system parts.
**Public question:** Why rebuild the same regulated workflow from scratch every time?
**Novice path:** Choose a regulated situation and see the recurring building blocks it needs in plain language.
**Expert depth:** jurisdiction/versioning, controls, policy-as-code boundaries, evidence standards, approval patterns, validation and change management.
**A technological ceiling:** versioned component registry; machine-readable control patterns; jurisdiction/context metadata; dependency and compatibility graph; policy-change propagation; validation harness; provenance; composable workflow generator; rule testing; implementation examples. Legal claims remain sourced and jurisdiction-scoped.
**B-Web ceiling:** interactive component wall: assemble a regulated workflow from reusable pieces, then change jurisdiction/context and watch which components become invalid or require review.
**B-LinkedIn:** dozens of regulated workflows collapse into the same recurring system parts.

## 045 — What Is It Actually Like to Get Cannabis Care? — REFRAME Cannabis Patient Journey Mapper
**Plain subtitle:** Map what a patient has to do, understand, pay for, wait for, and overcome from first question through ongoing use.
**Public question:** What does cannabis access look like from the patient's side rather than the market's side?
**Novice path:** Choose a patient situation and location, or build a journey step by step. See barriers, costs, confusing moments, and missing support.
**Expert depth:** eligibility, clinical interaction, product access, state rules, payment, transportation, stigma, accessibility, monitoring, discontinuation.
**A technological ceiling:** jurisdiction-aware pathway graph; geospatial access data where available; cost/time modeling; barrier ontology; scenario comparison; accessibility and transportation overlays; provenance and update tracking; patient-controlled qualitative input; privacy-preserving local/session modes; no individualized medical treatment recommendations.
**B-Web ceiling:** interactive patient journey map where changing location, mobility, cost, caregiver access, or eligibility visibly reroutes the path.
**B-LinkedIn:** a simple “buy cannabis” funnel unfolds into the much longer care/access journey people actually experience.

## 046 — What Does This Island Depend On? — REFRAME Island Systems Dependency Mapper
**Plain subtitle:** See which outside systems keep food, energy, medicine, transportation, communications, and other essentials working—and what happens when one fails.
**Public question:** What looks local but actually depends on something far away?
**Novice path:** Choose an essential need or disruption. Watch dependencies and likely cascading effects.
**Expert depth:** critical infrastructure, supply chains, redundancy, centrality, failure cascades, recovery, uncertainty, local capacity.
**A technological ceiling:** multilayer dependency graph; geospatial network model; public/open infrastructure and logistics data; graph centrality/criticality; Monte Carlo or scenario-based failure propagation where data supports it; uncertainty bands; recovery and substitution modeling; temporal scenarios; provenance.
**B-Web ceiling:** interactive map/network where the visitor cuts a port, cable, supplier, route, or service and watches consequences propagate spatially and across sectors.
**B-LinkedIn:** an island map slowly reveals the invisible off-island lines keeping daily life running.

## 047 — Will This Work Here? — REFRAME Hawaiʻi Localization / Implementation Framework
**Plain subtitle:** Test whether a policy, technology, service, or program that worked somewhere else can actually work in Hawaiʻi—or another specific place.
**Public question:** “It worked somewhere else” is not the same as “it will work here.” What has to be different?
**Novice path:** Name the idea and the place. Answer a few concrete questions about people, infrastructure, law, cost, geography, language, workforce, and access. Get the biggest implementation barriers and adaptations first.
**Expert depth:** jurisdictional constraints, cultural context, infrastructure readiness, labor capacity, procurement, geography, implementation science, stakeholder dependencies.
**A technological ceiling:** place profile/knowledge graph; geospatial layers; policy and infrastructure retrieval; constraint graph; scenario engine; comparable-place matching; evidence/provenance; uncertainty; participatory input; adaptation recommendations with traceable rationale.
**B-Web ceiling:** move the same intervention between places and watch its constraint map change; spatial layers and dependency graph update in real time.
**B-LinkedIn:** one “proven” solution crosses an ocean and immediately collides with the conditions it ignored.

## 048 — What Does This Rule Feel Like in Real Life? — REFRAME Governance Experience Assessment Tool
**Plain subtitle:** Compare what a rule says on paper with what people actually experience when they live under it.
**Public question:** Is the policy working the way its authors think it is?
**Novice path:** Choose or describe a rule and record what happens to a person trying to follow it. See friction, uncertainty, burden, dignity, access, recourse, and unintended effects.
**Expert depth:** administrative burden, procedural justice, implementation gaps, recourse, compliance experience, qualitative/quantitative evidence.
**A technological ceiling:** policy-to-process parsing; experience-journey mapping; structured qualitative coding; friction and burden metrics; subgroup comparison with privacy safeguards; policy/process gap graph; complaint/recourse mapping; change-over-time analysis; evidence provenance; mixed-methods dashboard.
**B-Web ceiling:** split-screen paper rule vs lived path; visitor changes one procedural requirement and watches burden/recourse effects propagate.
**B-LinkedIn:** the policy remains clean on the left while the lived experience on the right becomes increasingly difficult.

## 049 — What Works for You? — REFRAME Personal & Cannabis Sensory Profile Engine
**Plain subtitle:** Build a private profile of sensory preferences, sensitivities, context, goals, and past responses without reducing everything to a strain name.
**Public question:** What patterns in your own experience matter more than a product label?
**Novice path:** Record simple observations about context, senses, goals, and responses. See patterns over time without receiving medical advice.
**Expert depth:** within-person data, uncertainty, context effects, confounding, composition metadata, privacy, cautious personalization.
**A technological ceiling:** local-first encrypted personal profile; structured sensory/context schema; temporal N-of-1 records; pattern detection with uncertainty; Bayesian or hierarchical personal updating where warranted; product-composition import when reliable; explainable similarity; confound prompts; privacy controls/export; no diagnostic or treatment claims.
**B-Web ceiling:** interactive sensory/context constellation that reorganizes as the visitor changes setting, goal, prior response, or product composition.
**B-LinkedIn:** the strain name shrinks while the much richer context around the person expands.

## 050 — How Is This Space Affecting You? — REFRAME Neuroaesthetic Evidence & Decision-Support System
**Plain subtitle:** Explore how light, sound, layout, color, nature, crowding, and other features of a place may affect attention, comfort, stress, and experience—and how strong the evidence actually is.
**Public question:** What is the room doing to the person in it?
**Novice path:** Describe or photograph a space and choose what you are trying to improve. Receive understandable observations, evidence strength, uncertainty, and changes worth testing.
**Expert depth:** sensory pathways, environmental psychology, neuroaesthetics evidence quality, measurement, individual variation, causal limits.
**A technological ceiling:** multimodal scene analysis where privacy allows; spatial feature extraction; evidence retrieval/provenance; claim-strength grading; user-context model; recommendation engine constrained by evidence quality; counterfactual design alternatives; optional sensor/environmental data; personalization and uncertainty; experiment-generation link to later N-of-1 build. Avoid overstating neuroscience from weak associative evidence.
**B-Web ceiling:** interactive room where light/sound/layout/nature parameters can be manipulated while evidence confidence and predicted experiential tradeoffs update; accessible non-3D control surface alongside spatial/WebGL representation when warranted.
**B-LinkedIn:** an ordinary room is progressively annotated with the sensory inputs people normally stop noticing.
