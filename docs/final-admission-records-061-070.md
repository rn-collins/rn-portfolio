# Final Admission Records — Builds 061–070

Controlling standards: general-public comprehension; expert depth on demand; each A and B must reach its highest warranted technological ceiling; “memory” and “archive” do not justify a public slot unless the system helps someone recover context, compare evidence, make a decision, continue care, act, or avoid repeating failure.

## 061 — Why Did We Decide That? — REFRAME Founder Decision Memory System
**Plain subtitle:** Recover the evidence, assumptions, alternatives, disagreements, and outcomes behind an important decision.
**Public question:** Why do teams keep re-deciding questions they already answered?
**Novice path:** Record a decision in plain language, what you knew then, alternatives considered, and what happened. Later, search or reopen it and compare assumptions with outcomes.
**Expert depth:** decision provenance, dissent, assumptions, confidence, reversible/irreversible choices, post-decision review, version history.
**A technological ceiling:** structured decision-object model; semantic retrieval; evidence/provenance graph; assumption extraction with human confirmation; temporal versioning; outcome linkage; contradiction/change detection; scheduled assumption review; decision similarity and duplicate-decision detection; counterfactual comparison; local/private modes; explainable retrieval.
**B-Web ceiling:** interactive decision time machine: scrub from “what we knew then” to “what we know now,” with assumptions changing color as evidence arrives; branch alternate paths without pretending counterfactual certainty.
**B-LinkedIn:** a team debates the same question again until the original decision record reappears with its forgotten reasoning.
**Saveable payload:** `DECISION → WHY → ASSUMPTIONS → EVIDENCE → OUTCOME → REVIEW`.
**Sequence contribution:** decision memory + temporal assumption infrastructure.

## 062 — The Interview Is More Than a Transcript — REFRAME Interview Archive & Knowledge Graph
**Plain subtitle:** Turn conversations into people, claims, themes, evidence, contradictions, relationships, and follow-up questions.
**Public question:** What useful knowledge disappears when an interview becomes a text file and nothing more?
**Novice path:** Upload/paste an interview. See key people, claims, themes, unresolved questions, connections, and contradictions, with every extraction linked back to the source passage.
**Expert depth:** qualitative coding, speaker attribution, entity resolution, claim provenance, thematic analysis, uncertainty, cross-interview synthesis.
**A technological ceiling:** diarization/transcript ingestion where audio is permitted; speaker/entity resolution; structured annotation; claim extraction; embeddings + hybrid retrieval; knowledge graph; contradiction and agreement detection; source-span provenance; temporal/theme clustering; human correction; multimodal links to audio/video timestamps; privacy/redaction controls.
**B-Web ceiling:** transcript transforms into a living network; selecting any node snaps back to the exact sentence/audio moment that supports it; cross-interview themes emerge dynamically.
**B-LinkedIn:** a transcript collapses, then re-expands as a network of what the conversation actually contains.
**Saveable payload:** `PERSON → CLAIM → EVIDENCE → THEME → CONTRADICTION → FOLLOW-UP`.
**Sequence contribution:** interview annotation + source-linked knowledge graph.

## 063 — Research That Stays Alive — REFRAME Living Research Repository
**Plain subtitle:** Keep questions, evidence, claims, gaps, updates, methods, and outputs connected instead of burying them in folders.
**Public question:** What would research look like if it knew when its own conclusions became stale?
**Novice path:** Start with a question. Add sources and notes. The system shows what is supported, unresolved, contradictory, outdated, or ready to become an output.
**Expert depth:** evidence synthesis, review methods, provenance, update surveillance, claim state, reproducibility, research gaps.
**A technological ceiling:** research-object graph; claim/source provenance; semantic + lexical retrieval; deduplication; evidence-state machine; contradiction detection; freshness/obsolescence monitoring; source update alerts; structured extraction; methodology/version records; reproducible synthesis pipelines; human verification queues; citation export/API; optional agentic research assistance bounded by provenance and approval.
**B-Web ceiling:** research graph visibly evolves when a new source arrives; claims move from unresolved → supported → contested → stale; user can time-travel through evidence states.
**B-LinkedIn:** a “finished” research folder is frozen while the evidence outside it keeps changing; the repository wakes up and reconnects them.
**Saveable payload:** `QUESTION → SOURCE → CLAIM → GAP → UPDATE → OUTPUT`.
**Sequence contribution:** living evidence graph + freshness monitoring.

## 064 — An Industry With Amnesia — REFRAME Cannabis Institutional Memory Archive
**Plain subtitle:** Recover the policy, legal, clinical, market, community, and historical knowledge cannabis keeps losing between cycles.
**Public question:** How much cannabis policy and market knowledge gets rediscovered because nobody preserved the last cycle?
**Novice path:** Search a question such as “What happened the last time this policy was proposed?” and receive a sourced timeline, key actors, outcomes, and what was learned.
**Expert depth:** jurisdiction, legal status, institutional actors, market shifts, patient/community impacts, source authority, archival gaps.
**A technological ceiling:** heterogeneous archive ingestion; OCR only where needed; entity/time/jurisdiction extraction; knowledge graph; timeline reconstruction; semantic retrieval; source provenance; policy/version linkage; archived-web/document preservation metadata; contradiction and gap detection; geographic comparison; update monitoring; rights/permissions metadata.
**B-Web ceiling:** interactive regulatory/market memory timeline where recurring proposals and forgotten outcomes line up across years/jurisdictions; graph reveals cycles of institutional forgetting.
**B-LinkedIn:** the industry announces a “new” idea while the same idea appears repeatedly behind it in older documents.
**Saveable payload:** `WHAT HAPPENED → WHO KNEW → WHAT CHANGED → WHAT WAS FORGOTTEN`.
**Sequence contribution:** domain archive + timeline/jurisdiction memory infrastructure.

## 065 — Care Between the Sessions — KEEP / DEEPEN Psychedelic Care Continuity Record
**Plain subtitle:** Preserve the information and handoffs that matter before, during, and after psychedelic care without turning one session into the whole story.
**Public question:** What gets lost when preparation, session, integration, referrals, risks, and supports live in different places?
**Novice path:** Explore a fictional/sample care journey and see what information needs to travel, what should remain private, who needs it, and when.
**Expert depth:** consent, clinical boundaries, referral/handoff, longitudinal monitoring, adverse events, care teams, jurisdiction, privacy, record governance.
**A technological ceiling:** consent-governed longitudinal record; fine-grained data segmentation; role/context-based access; interoperable healthcare standards where appropriate (e.g. FHIR-compatible mapping without claiming universal interoperability); handoff workflow; provenance/version history; patient-controlled permissions; secure messaging/reference architecture; offline/emergency summaries; auditability; safety escalation. No treatment recommendation engine unless separately validated and clinically governed.
**B-Web ceiling:** longitudinal care path with information packets moving—or failing to move—across preparation/session/integration/referral boundaries; user adjusts consent and sees information flow change.
**B-LinkedIn:** one psychedelic “session” expands into the continuity architecture surrounding it.
**Saveable payload:** `PREPARE → SESSION → INTEGRATE → REFER → FOLLOW → CONTINUE`.
**Sequence contribution:** consent-governed longitudinal handoff record.

## 066 — What Does the Legal Team Already Know? — REFRAME Legal Knowledge Graph / Institutional Memory System
**Plain subtitle:** Connect matters, authorities, arguments, documents, experts, outcomes, workflows, and lessons so legal knowledge does not disappear into folders.
**Public question:** Before starting from scratch, what has this legal organization already learned about a similar problem?
**Novice path:** Ask a plain-language question and see prior matters, authorities, arguments, outcomes, and lessons—with clear source links and confidentiality boundaries.
**Expert depth:** privilege/confidentiality, matter context, authority status, argument lineage, expert history, work product, ethical walls, jurisdiction.
**A technological ceiling:** legal ontology/knowledge graph; hybrid retrieval; matter/entity resolution; authority verification inherited from 059; graph-aware RAG with mandatory citations; privilege and ethical-wall enforcement; temporal/jurisdiction filters; contradiction/treatment updates; expertise routing; outcome/argument linkage; human validation; audit trail; local/private model options where warranted.
**B-Web ceiling:** disconnected matter folders assemble into a graph; user asks one question and can trace an answer through prior matter → argument → authority → result while restricted nodes remain visibly sealed.
**B-LinkedIn:** “the firm knows” is shown to mean people and folders—until the knowledge becomes connected.
**Saveable payload:** `MATTER → ISSUE → ARGUMENT → AUTHORITY → OUTCOME → LESSON`.
**Sequence contribution:** permission-aware legal graph + graph-grounded retrieval.

## 067 — What Should Your AI Know About You? — REFRAME Personal Knowledge Model & Digital Identity Layer
**Plain subtitle:** Decide what an AI system may know, infer, remember, forget, and use about you.
**Public question:** A useful personal AI needs context—but who controls that context and its boundaries?
**Novice path:** Add simple facts/preferences/roles and choose who or what may use each. See explicit knowledge, inferred knowledge, uncertain knowledge, permissions, and expiry.
**Expert depth:** identity/context schemas, consent, provenance, inference governance, data minimization, context separation, portability, purpose limitation.
**A technological ceiling:** local-first encrypted personal knowledge graph; explicit vs inferred edge types; provenance/confidence; granular permissions/purpose/expiry; compartmentalized contexts; user-editable memory; conflict resolution; semantic retrieval; portability; on-device inference where feasible; privacy-preserving synchronization; inference disclosure; deletion propagation.
**B-Web ceiling:** interactive personal-context constellation; toggle permission/purpose/expiry and watch which AI capabilities gain/lose access; inferred nodes are visually distinct and challengeable.
**B-LinkedIn:** a “digital twin” rapidly accumulates assumptions until the user starts drawing the boundaries it should have had from the beginning.
**Saveable payload:** `KNOW / INFER / REMEMBER / SHARE / FORGET / ASK FIRST`.
**Sequence contribution:** governed personal knowledge graph + consent/context layer.

## 068 — Let Your AI Act—Without Giving It Your Life — REFRAME AI Digital Twin Action Runtime
**Plain subtitle:** Let a personal AI prepare and perform bounded actions while showing what it knows, what it can do, and when it needs approval.
**Public question:** How can an AI move from knowing context to taking useful action without silently acquiring unlimited authority?
**Novice path:** Choose a sample task. Watch the AI prepare it, identify required context/tools, ask for approval at meaningful points, execute in sandbox/simulation, and leave a readable action record.
**Expert depth:** tool permissions, least privilege, plans, memory boundaries, approval policies, transaction risk, reversibility, agent evaluation, auditability.
**A technological ceiling:** agent runtime over 067’s governed context graph; capability-based tool permissions; planner/executor separation; sandboxed tool simulation; consequence-aware approval gates; reversible actions/compensation where possible; policy engine; provenance/action ledger; trace evaluation inherited from 039; stop/escalate inherited from 040; secrets isolation; human-readable previews/diffs; transaction signing only where appropriate.
**B-Web ceiling:** user grants an agent progressively stronger capabilities and can watch its reachable action graph expand; high-consequence edges remain locked until explicit approval.
**B-LinkedIn:** “AI that knows me” becomes “AI that can act for me,” and the screen fills with the permissions that distinction requires.
**Saveable payload:** `CONTEXT → PLAN → PREVIEW → APPROVE → ACT → RECORD → REVERSE`.
**Sequence contribution:** governed personal-agent runtime + approval/action ledger.

## 069 — A Law Passed. Now What? — KEEP / DEEPEN Implementation-Aware Legal Tracker
**Plain subtitle:** Track whether a new law can actually become real through rules, budgets, systems, contracts, staffing, deadlines, and implementation.
**Public question:** Why does “the law changed” so often fail to tell us what actually changes next?
**Novice path:** Choose a law/policy. See the steps between enactment and lived implementation, who owns them, what is complete, blocked, funded, delayed, or unknown.
**Expert depth:** effective dates, rulemaking, appropriations, procurement, agency capacity, litigation, guidance, systems change, implementation evidence.
**A technological ceiling:** legislative/regulatory source ingestion; provision-to-obligation extraction with verification; implementation dependency graph; agency/entity resolution; milestone/state machine; budget/procurement linkage; deadline extraction; official-source change monitoring; geospatial/jurisdiction overlays; evidence/provenance; uncertainty; alerts; scenario/readiness modeling; human legal verification.
**B-Web ceiling:** enactment date appears as the beginning—not the end—of an interactive dependency path; visitor can expose funding, rulemaking, procurement, systems, staff, and litigation blockers.
**B-LinkedIn:** a celebratory “SIGNED INTO LAW” stamp lands, then dozens of implementation steps unfold beneath it.
**Saveable payload:** `ENACTED ≠ IMPLEMENTED: RULES → MONEY → PEOPLE → SYSTEMS → DELIVERY`.
**Sequence contribution:** implementation dependency graph + official-source monitoring.

## 070 — Legalization Is a Lifecycle — KEEP / DEEPEN Psychedelic Regulatory Lifecycle Tracker
**Plain subtitle:** Follow psychedelic reform from proposal and vote through rulemaking, licensing, implementation, enforcement, litigation, evaluation, and revision.
**Public question:** What happens after the headline says psychedelics were “legalized”?
**Novice path:** Choose a jurisdiction and see where reform actually is in the lifecycle, what comes next, who controls it, and what remains unresolved.
**Expert depth:** ballot/legislation, agency rulemaking, licensing, professional regulation, local implementation, enforcement, litigation, evaluation, revision.
**A technological ceiling:** jurisdictional regulatory knowledge graph; official-source ingestion/monitoring; lifecycle state machine; provision/rule/license linkage; entity resolution; timeline and dependency graph; litigation/enforcement events; geospatial layers; comparison across jurisdictions; provenance/version history; implementation readiness inherited from 069; alerts and uncertainty flags.
**B-Web ceiling:** interactive lifecycle wheel/timeline that refuses to stop at “legalized”; user compares jurisdictions and sees them occupy different regulatory states with different dependencies.
**B-LinkedIn:** the word LEGALIZED appears as a finish line, then moves backward to become only one milestone in a much longer path.
**Saveable payload:** `REFORM → RULES → LICENSES → DELIVERY → ENFORCEMENT → EVALUATION → REVISION`.
**Sequence contribution:** regulated lifecycle state model + jurisdiction comparison infrastructure.

# Batch verdict
All ten can justify public slots **only in these decision/action-oriented expressions**. None may ship as “a knowledge graph,” “an archive,” “a digital twin,” or “a tracker” whose primary value is storing information.

The technological progression inside this phase is intentional:
061 decision memory → 062 source-linked conversation knowledge → 063 living research → 064 domain institutional memory → 065 continuity across care → 066 permission-aware institutional legal memory → 067 governed personal memory → 068 governed personal action → 069 implementation intelligence → 070 full regulatory lifecycle intelligence.

## Boundary checks before canon freeze
- 061 is decision reasoning memory; 063 is research evidence state; do not collapse them.
- 062 is interview-derived knowledge; it can feed 063 but should remain a distinct capture/analysis experience.
- 064 is cannabis institutional/history memory, not another general research repository.
- 065 is continuity-of-care architecture, not an archive.
- 066 must enforce legal permissions and authority status; otherwise it is merely 063 with legal documents.
- 067 is user-controlled context; 068 is bounded action. Knowledge does not imply authority.
- 069 is implementation after a legal/policy act; 070 is a domain-specific end-to-end psychedelic regulatory lifecycle and must inherit rather than duplicate 069.
