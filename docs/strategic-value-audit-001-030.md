# RN Systems Lab — Strategic Value Audit, Builds 001–030

**Purpose:** Decide which technically valid builds deserve scarce public numbered slots.

A public build must do more than teach an engineering capability. It should help create the behavioral chain:

**STOP → UNDERSTAND → SAVE → CLICK → USE → REMEMBER RN → CONTACT RN**

## Scoring standard

Each build is scored 1–5 on nine dimensions (45 points total):

1. RN fit — does this make sense coming from RN specifically?
2. Audience demand — is there a live problem people recognize?
3. Saveability — can B leave an intellectual souvenir worth returning to?
4. Visual potential — can the underlying mechanism become a compelling visual build?
5. Utility / adoption — can A deliver useful value with low friction?
6. Technical progression — does it add meaningful capability?
7. Distinctiveness — is it more than a generic developer demo?
8. Click potential — does B create a credible reason to visit A?
9. Commercial gravity — could the right viewer plausibly want RN's help afterward?

### Dispositions
- **40–45 Anchor:** protect the slot; signature public build.
- **35–39 Keep:** strong public build, may need reframing.
- **30–34 Reframe:** capability may stay, public expression must become more RN-specific.
- **≤29 Demote/replace:** build as Lab infrastructure or backlog unless a much stronger public expression is found.

## Executive finding for 001–030

The first 30 currently mix two different things:

- **public intellectual products** that can become recognizable RN work; and
- **engineering primitives** that the Lab absolutely needs but that should not automatically consume one of 100 public slots.

The strongest early public territory is human/AI judgment, legal work, regulated-market handoffs, evidence, consent/privacy, decision architecture, and accessibility. Generic infrastructure such as authentication, API gateways, raw ingestion, and generic state machinery should usually be built invisibly in the Lab and surfaced publicly only through a problem with stronger RN-specific stakes.

## Replacement-candidate directions

These are directions, not frozen titles. Candidate replacements should be researched/scored before insertion.

### Candidate R-A — AI Procurement / Vendor Claim X-Ray
A tool that takes an AI vendor claim or procurement pitch and exposes what must be verified: data, evaluation, human oversight, privacy, failure modes, evidence, lock-in, liability, and operational fit.

Why it fits: AI implementation × law/governance × purchasing decisions; highly saveable and commercially relevant.

### Candidate R-B — Policy-to-Workflow Translator
Turns a statute, policy, institutional rule, or AI policy into concrete actors, decisions, evidence, deadlines, exceptions, and workflow changes.

Why it fits: directly bridges law and implementation rather than stopping at explanation.

### Candidate R-C — Evidence Strength / Claim Defensibility Mapper
Lets a user enter a consequential claim and map what evidence supports it, what is inference, what is missing, how fresh it is, and what would change confidence.

Why it fits: research, law, science communication, AI outputs, governance and journalism all converge here.

### Candidate R-D — Institutional Friction Detector
Maps where an institution's formal policy and actual lived process diverge, including handoffs, wait states, missing ownership and hidden work.

Why it fits: institutions, future of work, government, regulated markets, user experience and organizational design.

### Candidate R-E — Place-Sensitive Technology Readiness Assessment
Tests whether a proposed technology assumes connectivity, staffing, culture, language, infrastructure, procurement or institutional conditions that are not true in the place where it will be deployed.

Why it fits: Hawaiʻi/island systems, localization, technology implementation, governance and future-of-place work.
