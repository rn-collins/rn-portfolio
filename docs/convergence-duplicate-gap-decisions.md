## 2. Gap challenger tournament for the freed slot
The gap register identified several possible underrepresented capabilities. Each challenger must be a strong public product, not a curriculum checkbox.

### Challenger A — Contract Consequence Explorer
Public job: “What does this contract clause actually change for each side?”
Novice path: paste/select a plain contract or sample clause; see obligations, rights, deadlines, dependencies, money/risk triggers, termination/renewal, missing/ambiguous terms and questions to raise. Not legal advice.
Technological ceiling: contract/document parsing; clause segmentation/classification; defined-term/entity graph; obligation/right/condition/deadline extraction; cross-reference graph; semantic diff/redline; scenario/consequence propagation; contradiction/ambiguity detection; provenance to exact text; jurisdiction/context metadata; human-review mode; evaluation against annotated contracts. Later extension can compare versions or model operational consequences.
B-Web: contract becomes a living obligation graph; change one clause and watch downstream rights, duties, dates and risk paths recompute.
Why it fills a gap: concrete transactional/business-law artifact, broadly understandable, technically rich, directly connected to legal/contract systems without duplicating regulatory tracking.
Verdict: STRONG.

### Challenger B — Deal / Ownership Structure Simulator
Public job: “If ownership, investment, dilution, control or economics change, who gets what and who controls what?”
Technological ceiling: cap-table/ownership graph, waterfall scenarios, dilution, option/convertible mechanics where scoped, voting/control rights, scenario comparison, sensitivity analysis, provenance. Legal/financial disclaimers and no personalized investment advice.
B-Web: interactive ownership topology and economic/control outcomes recompute as terms move.
Why it fills a gap: transactional + founder + ownership + quantitative modeling.
Verdict: STRONG, but overlaps creator ownership/IP/benefit-sharing territory and requires careful legal/financial scope.

### Challenger C — Accessible Interface Transformation Lab
Public job: “What happens to this interface when the user cannot see, hear, point, read, process, or connect the way its designer assumed?”
Technological ceiling: real DOM/component ingestion where permitted; automated accessibility tree inspection; keyboard/focus simulation; contrast/color-vision transformations; motion reduction; text complexity transformation; screen-reader-oriented semantic representation; bandwidth/device emulation; multimodal alternatives; repair suggestions and before/after runnable UI.
B-Web: one interface transforms live under different access conditions, then visitor repairs it.
Why it fills a gap: makes accessibility a working technology rather than QA rubric.
Verdict: VERY STRONG, but overlaps 030 Inclusive Experience Simulator. Better used to deepen 030 than consume a new slot.

### Challenger D — Multilingual Meaning Preservation Lab
Public job: “Did this message keep the same meaning when it crossed language and culture?”
Technological ceiling: multilingual models, terminology/claim anchors, back-translation as one signal not truth, semantic equivalence, locale/context profiles, human review, ambiguity detection, cultural adaptation notes, provenance and evaluation across language pairs.
B-Web: meaning anchors remain fixed while wording/cultural framing transforms; drift becomes visible.
Why it fills a gap: actual multilingual runtime capability.
Verdict: STRONG, but naturally extends 002 Multi-Audience Messaging Architecture. Deepen 002 with multilingual/cross-cultural mode rather than duplicate it.

### Challenger E — Collaborative Decision Room
Public job: “Can a group make a decision without hiding disagreement, evidence, assumptions or tradeoffs?”
Technological ceiling: multi-user real-time collaboration/CRDTs; structured options/criteria; evidence/provenance; anonymous or named input; disagreement maps; argument graphs; scenario comparison; decision rules; audit trail; facilitation; accessibility; asynchronous participation.
B-Web: multiple simulated/real participants move evidence and priorities; consensus/disagreement topology changes live.
Why it fills a gap: true multi-user collaboration is underrepresented and becomes infrastructure for 100’s collaborative decision room.
Verdict: VERY STRONG.

## Winner for freed slot: Collaborative Decision Room
Reason: it fills the clearest capability gap without being merely an extension of an existing build. It introduces real multi-user collaborative state, disagreement representation, structured deliberation and CRDT/realtime infrastructure that later builds—including 084 benefit sharing, 095 governance experience, 099 commons governance and 100 island decision room—can inherit.

Contract Consequence Explorer remains the highest-priority challenger if another duplicate is eliminated during final sequencing. It should also be considered as a future build only if it beats an incumbent; do not expand above 100 by default.
